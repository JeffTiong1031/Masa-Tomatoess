# Meals photo story — human verification

**Branch:** `feat/meals-photo-story`
**Spec:** [2026-08-23-meals-photo-story-design.md](../specs/2026-08-23-meals-photo-story-design.md)
**Plan:** [2026-08-23-meals-photo-story.md](../plans/2026-08-23-meals-photo-story.md)

457 automated tests cover the pure logic. **Nothing below is covered by them.** The
camera, the uploads, both AI endpoints and every screen were verified by code
review only — this checklist is the whole of their testing.

Work through it on a real phone against the deployed host, not on the desktop.

---

## Before anything works

- [ ] The app is deployed and reachable over **HTTPS**. Phone cameras refuse to
      open on plain HTTP, so this is not optional.
- [ ] Spec §4's SQL has been run in Supabase: `meal_entries`, `meal_days`,
      `meal_reviews`. The `/cycle` tables from the 2026-08-16 spec are still
      pending too — run both in one sitting.
- [ ] A storage bucket named `meal-photos` exists with **public listing disabled**.
- [ ] `GEMINI_API_KEY` is set in the host's environment, with no `NEXT_PUBLIC_`
      prefix.

Everything from "The estimate" onward fails without the key — the app returns 503
and shows meals with no calories. That is by design, but you will not be testing
the AI half until the key exists.

---

## The photo

- [ ] Open `/meals`. The month grid renders with the weekday columns aligned and
      today ringed.
- [ ] Tap the camera button. Your phone's normal camera opens.
- [ ] Take a photo of a meal. It appears in today's square **within a second or
      two**.
- [ ] Check the network panel: the grid loads **thumbnails** (~15KB each), not
      full photos. Thirty full-size photos would be ~9MB per page view.
- [ ] The photo is right way up and not obviously distorted.

## Offline

Put the phone in airplane mode for these.

- [ ] Photograph a meal. It appears on today's square immediately, **marked as
      waiting**.
- [ ] You get a message saying it saved on this phone and will upload later. You
      are never left unsure whether it saved.
- [ ] Turn signal back on, reopen the app. The photo uploads and the waiting
      marker clears.
- [ ] Check the `meal-photos` bucket: exactly **two** files for that meal (full
      and thumbnail), not four. *(Regression: a failed insert after a successful
      upload used to strand blobs and re-upload a fresh pair on each retry.)*

## The estimate

- [ ] Photograph a real meal. A card appears naming the dish with a calorie
      number.
- [ ] Smaller / Larger change the number; tapping Smaller then Larger returns you
      to the original figure rather than compounding.
- [ ] Typing your own number overrides the buttons.
- [ ] **Cancel** closes the card and leaves the meal saved without a number.
- [ ] **Discard** removes the meal *and* both its files from the bucket.
- [ ] Save writes it into the day.

- [ ] Photograph something that is **not food**, or a very dark bowl. The card
      flips: it leads with "I can't tell what this is", offers no pre-filled
      number, and **Save stays disabled** until you type both a dish and a
      figure. *(Regression: the flip used to reorder the fields but still leave
      the model's guess pre-filled and savable.)*
- [ ] Clear the calorie field entirely. Save stays disabled — a blank does not
      save as zero.

- [ ] Open a day, find a meal showing "not counted yet", tap **Tap to estimate**.
      It requests an estimate and opens the confirm card.

## The story

- [ ] Have Rachel photograph a meal on her phone. It appears on your grid.
- [ ] Tap a day both of you ate on. The sheet shows your photos **interleaved by
      time**, not grouped by person.
- [ ] Each caption sits **beneath** its photo. No text is drawn over any
      photograph anywhere in the app.
- [ ] Tap a caption. The editor opens; change the dish, the number and the slot,
      and save. The story reflects it.
- [ ] Clear the dish and try to save. Save is disabled. *(Regression: an empty
      dish used to silently discard the whole edit, calorie change included.)*
- [ ] Delete a meal. It disappears, the sheet stays open, and its files are gone
      from the bucket.
- [ ] Delete a day's only meal. The sheet shows "Nothing photographed on this
      day" rather than breaking.

## The 4am day

The point of these: a food day runs 04:00 to 04:00 so a late supper counts to the
day you were still living.

- [ ] Photograph something after midnight but before 4am. It lands on
      **yesterday's** square, not today's.
- [ ] Open that day's story. The supper appears **last**, after dinner — not first,
      above breakfast. *(Regression: sorting by clock time put 01:00 at the top.)*
- [ ] In that same window, today's ring is on the correct square and the week card
      still counts the supper. *(Regression: the boundary used to apply to writes
      but not to "which week is it now", so a 1am meal appeared in no total.)*

## The nudge

- [ ] Leave yesterday incomplete. Before 8am, no card appears.
- [ ] After 8am, a card names the missing meals.
- [ ] Type a description into the gap-fill. It lands on **yesterday**, not today.
- [ ] Fill one gap, then fill a second without touching the slot buttons. The
      second lands on the **correct** remaining slot. *(Regression: a stale
      selection used to file a second breakfast and never record lunch.)*
- [ ] Seal the day. The card disappears and the day joins the week total.
- [ ] Have Rachel *not* seal her day. Your nudge stays gone and hers still shows —
      seals are per person.

## The week

- [ ] The week card shows both names with separate totals and a day count.
- [ ] The seven bars are a **deep apricot**, not purple. *(Regression: they used a
      token that resolves to the calendar section's colour.)*
- [ ] Press **Review my week**. The write-up names your actual dishes and refers to
      the right number of days.
- [ ] Edit a meal in that week. The button changes to **Refresh** and an
      out-of-date note appears.
- [ ] Seal another day. The button changes to **Refresh** again. *(Regression:
      sealing used to leave the review looking current, locking you out of
      re-reviewing.)*
- [ ] Press it on an unchanged week. It returns the stored write-up with no
      network call.

## Month edges

- [ ] On the **1st of a month**, the nudge reads yesterday correctly rather than
      claiming every meal is missing. *(Regression: yesterday fell outside the
      fetched range.)*
- [ ] The grid's leading and trailing squares — the previous and next month's days
      that fill the first and last rows — show their photos, dimmed. *(Regression:
      they were outside the fetch and always looked empty.)*
- [ ] Page back several months with the arrows, then back to today. The week card
      still shows this week's real totals rather than zeros.

## Known and accepted

Not bugs — decisions on the record.

- **Anyone who reaches the public URL can read the database**, photos included.
  The anon key ships in the page source and every table policy is `using (true)`;
  the password gate hides screens, not data. Raised during design and accepted as
  not worth the effort for food photos. Spec §12.
- **Free-tier Gemini submissions are used by Google to improve their products**,
  paid-tier ones are not. A billing setting, not a code change. Covers Rachel's
  photos as well as yours.
- **Both AI endpoints are public and unauthenticated.** Size caps now stop an
  oversized request costing anything, but nothing stops someone who finds the URL
  from spending your Gemini quota. Your spec accepted strangers *reading* your
  data; it never considered them *spending your money*. **This one is still an
  open decision.**
- **No push notification.** The 8am nudge is a card you see when you open the app.
- **No daily calorie target.** The review works from patterns. Add one if the
  advice reads as too vague.
- Storage grows about **650MB a year** at three meals a day for two people; the
  free tier is 1GB.

## Still unverified after this checklist

- Whether Gemini's calorie numbers are *sensible* on real Malaysian food. The
  request shape is verified against the SDK's own types, but the model's
  behaviour is not. Expect to retune the prompt in
  `src/app/api/meals/estimate/route.ts` after a week of real photos.
- Whether the low-confidence threshold is set well. If it fires constantly you
  will be typing dishes by hand; if it never fires, bad guesses slip through.
